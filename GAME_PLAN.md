# In Waiting: Game Plan

Working title only. Status: mechanics draft, not yet tested on humans. **Development target: the opening of the game, covering 1 rival.**

## The pitch

You are a lady in waiting. Nobody looks at the help, and that is your weapon. Each month you schedule your days to keep your mistress in the prince's favour, dig up secrets, and block a rival's moves. At the Ball, your mistress waltzes with the prince in a card haggle for his heart. Every dance lowers his defences, every whisper seduces him, and when he finally falls, the rival falls with him.

- **Inspirations:** Persona and Princess Maker for the calendar, Potionomics for preparing all month and cashing it in during one showdown.
- **Our edge:** gameplay and writing. Art stays simple on purpose.
- **Scope:** the opening only, covering one rival from start to finish, built in Godot.

## Setting and story

This section is a draft for the writer to shape.

**What the game critiques:** the class system and nepotism. Everything else in the story should serve those two ideas.

**Humans only:** there are no elves, dwarves or other fantasy races. It's fantasy because the kingdom, its history and its cultures are invented. Magic must never solve intrigue, so the world's mystery comes from omens, saints and curses that nobody can prove.

**The kingdom:** a fantasy realm inspired by medieval Spain. Waltz, tango and flamenco are working names, because those dances came much later. The final game should give them in-world names.

**The helper:** an adult woman, enslaved, from a faraway island kingdom inspired by Indonesia, as a nod to the team's roots. Her mistress bought her to help her, giving her fine clothes and a real education and raising her status in life.

**Silent disdain:** nobody insults her out loud. Merchants from her homeland trade spices at court and are welcomed for their wealth, so people can claim her people are accepted. Those same merchants may look straight through her.

**Her disdain is her weapon:** people talk in front of her as if she isn't there. That's why she can spy, so the game's central mechanic and its theme are the same thing. This needs no new system, only writing.

**How society marks her:** a sealed bronze bracelet stamped with her household's crest, which only a notary can remove. Servants' clothing rules can add to this, with enslaved people barred from wearing dyed cloth.

**Why she stays loyal:** her mistress is on her side but doesn't have the power to change things yet. Only as queen could she free her and begin changing the law. The helper's work is about more than ambition: if her mistress wins, she can finally act.

**The masked ball:** the competition is a masked ball. A mask hides her face, and long gloves hide the bracelet. The prize buys her freedom, giving her a second path, and she never has to betray her mistress for that to matter.

**How nepotism shows up (no new systems needed):**

- **The masked ball is the one fair place.** With names hidden, only skill counts. That's why a servant can rise there, and it's the game's clearest statement on merit.
- **Each rival rises through family.** Rivals get harder because they're better connected, so their family's influence is what raises the prince's starting Defence. The last rival could be the king's own niece.
- **Servants do the work, and nobles take the credit.** The Steward runs a household for a lord who holds the title. A well-connected heir could hold a post the helper's trainer earned.
- **Connections try to buy the masked ball too.** A high-ranked dancer turns out to be a noble's son whose family has paid the judges, so even the fair place is under threat.
- **It can infect the dark ending.** Part of the corruption could be the queen handing out posts to the people who helped her, becoming what she set out to change.

**Where the dances come from:** every style began among ordinary people before the upper classes took it up. That's why servants are the ones who can teach them, and it's the class critique built into the dance system. These are real-world inspirations, and the kingdom can have its own versions and names.

| Style | Real-world roots | What it means in the game |
|---|---|---|
| **Flamenco** | Andalusia, shaped largely by the Romani people and other marginalised communities | A proud art from people the court despises, taught by the Laundress |
| **Waltz** | Country dances in Austria and southern Germany, once considered scandalous for its close hold | A peasant dance the nobility now calls refined, taught by the Steward |
| **Tango** | The working-class port districts of Buenos Aires and Montevideo, scorned before high society embraced it | A dance from the docks, taught by the Nursemaid |
| **Minuet** | The French court, often traced back to a country dance | The most aristocratic style, polished until its origins were forgotten, taught by the Seamstress |

**How each dance's movement justifies its role:**

| Style | Role | How the dance moves | Answers | Why it answers that stance | Possible move names from real steps |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Flamenco** | Attack | A fantasy couple's version that borrows from Sevillanas and Pasodoble. Partners face off and circle with stamping footwork and sharp arms. Your mistress is the matador and the prince is the bull | Guarded | She provokes a man who doesn't want to engage until he charges. Standing that close is why it's risky | Zapateado (stamping footwork), Vuelta (turn), Remate (closing flourish) |
| **Waltz** | Defence, buffs, healing | A steady closed hold, smooth turning and an even three-beat rhythm. Each partner supports the other's weight | Forward | A strong frame absorbs his push, and the steady rhythm lets her recover | Closed frame, Hesitation, Natural turn |
| **Tango** | Debuff | A close embrace with slow walks, sudden pauses, and steps that hook or displace a partner's leg. It takes away a partner's balance and space | Proud | It unbalances him and takes control of the dance, so he can't recover | Sacada (displacing step), Gancho (hook), Barrida (sweep) |
| **Minuet** | Finisher | Tiny, precise steps with a held gaze and brief hand touches. Partners circle, approach and pull away | Aloof | Its restraint draws him in, and there's nothing to counter because nothing improper has happened | Reverence (bow), Z-figure (its crossing path), Presented hand |

**Possible final payoff:** cutting off the bracelet. Later in the game, she could also bring her homeland's dance into the competition.

**Tone rules:**

- This is a fantasy that mirrors real life to critique it. It never glorifies slavery, and the writing should show its cost.
- Keep the helper out of all seduction content. That style belongs to the mistress, and the helper's enslaved status must never be sexualised.
- If the game draws on real Indonesian culture, have team members with those roots guide it.

## The goal: "one more day"

"Deeper than it looks" means the loop is addictive. Every other decision serves this. The trick is to stack short loops inside long ones, so there is always a reward coming soon and an unfinished goal pulling the player onward.

| Loop | Length | What the player chases | What pulls them onward |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Action** | Seconds | The stat jump, and what the action turned up | A big number, a growing streak, a secret that might complete a set |
| **Day** | 1 to 2 minutes | Making today's plan work | The bars drop overnight and the night ends on a tease |
| **Week** | 3 days | A good spin at the Court Event | Fortune's Wheel, whose odds you shaped all week |
| **Month** | About 30 minutes | Destroying the rival in the Ball haggle | One huge payoff, or a close loss you want to replay |
| **Across runs** | Hours | Beating the rival, then seeing the other ending | Replaying the same month knowing her plan, and chasing the good ending |

### Your three hooks, all in one game

- **The haggle is the big one, so it's the Ball.** The prince is who you're haggling with. You choose how far to push before he pulls a move on you. Winning him is also how you destroy the rival, so she's still the payoff.
- **Stat jumps run the daily loop.** Consecutive safe days build a streak that makes gains bigger, so numbers climb and the streak feels worth protecting.
- **Nearly finished relationships are the servants.** Each one shows progress toward their next gift and dance move, so there's always one almost done.

### The rules that make it stick

1. **Always leave something unfinished.** "One more Debt secret makes a Scandal" should be on screen at all times. An almost-finished set is what keeps people clicking.
2. **Never end a day on a calm note.** Each night closes with a tease: a rival move, a chance tomorrow, or a set one card short. Stopping there should feel wrong.
3. **Make results a reveal.** Actions play out one at a time. Bars move, a secret flips over, and the rival answers. Planning is the investment and the reveal is the payoff.
4. **Payoff every 3 days, not every 12.** Thirty-six actions is too long to wait for one reward, so Court Events add smaller peaks on the way to the Ball.
5. **The player must feel clever.** The best moments are ones they planned, like spotting the rival's move, blocking it, and gaining ground in the same turn.
6. **Luck adds drama, skill sets the odds.** Good play earns better chances, so a bad spin feels like a risk you took.
7. **Losing should point at the fix.** Show exactly how close it was and what would have closed the gap. Then make retrying instant.

## The rules

### A month

- 12 days, split into 4 weeks of 3 days. Each day has Morning, Afternoon and Evening.
- Plan all 3 actions at dawn, then watch them play out.
- 12 days instead of 30 is deliberate. Thirty days at 3 actions each is 90 decisions per rival, and that many would drag before the Ball. It can still be called a month in the story.

### Three bars, all with a safe zone

Bars start near the middle and drop every night, so they always need tending. The goal is to stay between 35 and 70, so too high is as dangerous as too low.

| Bar | Means | Too low | Too high |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Propriety** | What the court thinks of her | Scandal | Stiff and dull |
| **Allure** | What the prince feels | Forgotten | Improper |
| **Nerve** | How your mistress is holding up | She breaks | Overconfident, stops listening |

Every night a bar sits outside the zone adds 1 Strain. At 8 Strain, she collapses and the month ends in a bad ending.

**Streak:** each day all 3 bars end in the zone adds 10% to your gains, up to +50%. One bad night resets it.

### Suspicion

This is how close anyone is to catching you. Dirty work raises it and helping the servants lowers it. Higher Suspicion makes pressing riskier at the Ball, and at 100 you are caught, which is a bad ending.

### Stain

This tracks how dirty your hands are, and it decides your ending. Every dirty action adds to it, and unlike Suspicion it never goes down.

### Actions

| Action | Stain | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Etiquette drill | 0 | Propriety up, costs Allure and Nerve |
| Gown and beauty | 0 | Allure up, slightly improper |
| Tea and counsel | 0 | Nerve up, morning or evening |
| Arrange an encounter | 0 | Big Allure, costs Nerve, afternoon only. Reveals one of his stances for the Ball, and earns a Prince's Favour card if Allure is 40+ (2 at most) |
| Audience with the Queen Mother | 0 | Prince's defence -1, once a week, needs Propriety 50+ |
| Help a contact | 0 | Suspicion -12, and every 4 visits that contact gives you hearsay |
| Dance lesson | 0 | Afternoon only. Works toward that style's next move and counts as 1 visit with that trainer, but you're too sore for dirty actions tonight. Nerve -3 |
| Ask the staff | 0 | Shows the rival's moves for today and tomorrow |
| Intercept | 0 | Blocks a rival move you have already seen |
| Watch the rival | 1 | Shows her next 3 days, Suspicion +4 |
| Eavesdrop | 1 | +1 secret, +2 on Salon days, Suspicion +6 |
| Search her rooms | 3 | +2 secrets, reveals her weakness, evening only, Suspicion +15 |
| Blackmail | 3 | Spend 1 secret, +12 to your lowest bar, Suspicion +6 |
| Spread a rumour | 4 | Spend 1 secret, Prince's defence -2, Suspicion +9 |

**Contacts:** the Laundress (Lover), Steward (Debt), Seamstress (Faith) and Nursemaid (Family). Each one's hearsay carries that tag. Hearsay is weaker than a stolen secret but adds no Stain, which is the clean route.

Repeating an action within a week weakens its gains (100%, then 70%, then 40%), so no single routine wins.

### The rival

The rival never fights you directly. She works on the prince all month, and his **Defence** is the wall you must break at the Ball. It starts at 26. She makes 8 moves on hidden days, each hitting a bar or your Suspicion. A move that lands turns him further against you, adding +1 Defence. A blocked move takes 1 away, so every block is a 2-point swing.

### Dance lessons

The dances at the Ball are waltzes with the prince, and your servants are secretly the best dance teachers at court. You learn from them, then teach your mistress.

- **One lesson a day, in the Afternoon.** That's the same slot as encounters with the prince, so every afternoon asks whether to see him or prepare for the Ball.
- **Soreness is the cost.** After a lesson, your body hurts and you can't do dirty actions that evening. Evening is the only time you can search the rival's rooms, so every lesson gives up your best spying chance.
- **Lessons build the relationship.** Each one counts as a visit, so getting to know a trainer earns you moves and gossip.

Each style plays differently at the Ball.

| Style | Trainer | Role | How it plays | Moves (placeholder names and values) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Flamenco** | Laundress | Attack | Huge hits, but press risk rises for the rest of the dance | Heel stamp 5, Whirl 10, Final pose 16 |
| **Waltz** | Steward | Defence, buffs and healing | Lowers press risk, survives one of his moves, and heals. Graceful lead saves half your stake if he strikes, and lifts your lowest bar by 8 at Court Dances | Steady frame 5, Graceful lead 10, Promenade 15 |
| **Tango** | Nursemaid | Debuff | Lowers his Defence the moment it's played, safe from his moves | Close embrace 4, Sharp turn 7, The dip 12 |
| **Minuet** | Seamstress | Finisher | He can't counter it, and it hits harder the higher your Allure | Lingering glance 2, Soft whisper 8, Stolen touch 13, each plus Allure / 10 |

**Move progression**

Each style has 6 moves, and each move takes more lessons than the last.

| Move | 1st | 2nd | 3rd | 4th | 5th | 6th |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lessons for this move | 1 | 3 | 5 | 7 | 9 | 11 |
| Total lessons in that style | 1 | 4 | 9 | 16 | 25 | 36 |

- **Moves carry across all 5 months.** A month has only 12 afternoons, so one month can reach the 3rd move at best. The last moves are long-term goals.
- **You can't master everything.** Five months have at most 60 afternoons, and all four styles would take 144 lessons. Choosing who to train with is a real build decision.
- **Early moves are a taste, later moves are the prize.** A first move is a useful card, but only your 2nd move or better can answer the prince's stance.
- **Too many lessons has a cost.** Each afternoon spent training isn't spent on the bars, so overtraining can end in a collapse.

The prototype has the first 3 moves of each style, because that's all one month can reach.

**The Seamstress is a retired courtesan.** She has spent decades learning what makes powerful men weak, and now she mends gowns where no one looks at her. Her lessons are the below-the-belt option: each one costs 1 Stain and 2 Propriety, on top of the usual soreness. Minuet is the one style that pulls against the good ending.

### Court Events and Fortune's Wheel

At the end of days 3, 6 and 9, your mistress dances one short haggle with a noble, then you spin a wheel of 8 slices. Dances show up all month this way, so the Ball is a bigger version of something players already know.

- **The dance loads the wheel.** Score 12 or more for 2 extra lucky slices, or 6 or more for 1. If her partner catches her out, she's embarrassed in public and you lose 2.
- **Your month loads it too.** The wheel starts with 2 lucky slices. Each bar in the zone adds 1, and Suspicion under 30 adds 1.
- **Lucky results:** a free secret, a Prince's Favour, Suspicion -15, or Prince's defence -2.
- **Unlucky results:** Suspicion +12, your highest bar -12, Prince's defence +2, or a lost secret.
- **You can fight back.** After a bad result, spend a secret to spin again. It's another use for the same secrets you want at the Ball.

### The dance school

This is the helper's own story, built around the school's masked dance competition. Everyone competes masked and under a false name, which is the only reason a servant can enter. She sneaks out in the Evening and climbs the competition's ranks by beating one masked dancer at a time. She stays loyal to her mistress throughout. The prince isn't involved, because players must love her.

- **Reward:** beat the dancer at your rank and your best move permanently gets +3 value.
- **Risk:** sneaking out adds 4 Suspicion, and losing costs 2 Nerve.
- **Cost:** it uses your Evening, the best time for spying. After a lesson you can't do dirty actions anyway, so lesson-then-school makes a natural dance day.
- **Scores needed:** 4, 6, 8, 11, 14 and 18 for the 6 ranks.

Ranks and upgrades should carry between months, as moves do.

### The Ball: the haggle

The Ball works like a Potionomics competition. It's playable in `webapp/`, and these rules replace the older push-your-luck version.

**The goal:** there are 3 rounds of 3 waltzes, 9 in all. In each waltz, bring the prince's **Composure** below the rival's mark before the **Music** runs out. Every round is played, and a failed waltz only earns no Hearts.

**Each round**

1. **The rival dances first.** A short, skippable scene shows her 4 measures with a Charm number for each, so she visibly plays by your rules. How far she gets sets your gold mark for the round, and a second tick shows her pace during your waltz.
2. **You edit your Dance Card.** You can see his three stances for the round, then swap cards in or out, up to 14.
3. **You dance three waltzes.** Each measure, you draw 5 cards and get a limited number of steps, starting at 4 and rising to 5.
4. **He shows his next move in a thought bubble,** so you can attack or build Poise to block it.
5. **The Music starts at 10 and drains faster each measure** (1, then 2, then 3). Some cards add Music, so you can earn more time.

**Dance phrases**

Every card is a **Step**, a **Turn** or a **Pose**. Play them in that order and each next card in the phrase is free, so dancing well is how you avoid running out of steps. Finishing a phrase triggers a **Flourish**: a bonus Charm, and the couple replays the sequence.

**Draw cards help you out of a bad hand.** Each style's first move draws a card, and so do Quick step and Nerve's Grace. Keep draw limited, because too much of it combines with free phrase cards into a loop that makes the game trivial.

**Every card Charms him.** Even support cards deal a little Charm, so no hand feels wasted. Passing the mark should feel reachable, and the challenge is sweeping him to 0.

**Card wording:** cards use short effects in the style of Potionomics, such as "Charm 7" or "Poise +6". Charm means lowering his Composure. Each card shows a small picture of its dance pose, and the same drawings can become the card art.

**The prince talks and reacts:** his next move appears as a line of dialogue, and he responds to the cards you play. His face changes with each reaction and as his Composure falls. The three waltzes in each round have their own moods: polite, then flirtatious, then honest. All his lines are in `webapp/lines.js`, so the writer can replace them.

**His moves**

- **Takes the lead:** adds Fluster, less your Poise.
- **Collects himself:** recovers Composure.
- **Unsettles her:** adds a dead Flustered card to your deck.

**Fluster:** every 8 Fluster adds a dead card to your deck. At 24 she falters: she loses 4 Music, your phrase breaks, a Flustered card joins your deck, and Fluster drops to 12. The dance goes on. She recovers some Fluster between waltzes.

**Results and rewards**

| How the waltz ends | Hearts |
|---|---|
| Swept off his feet: Composure reaches 0 | 2 |
| A closer played below her mark | 1, and leftover Music calms Fluster |
| The Music ends below her mark | 1 |
| Above her mark when the Music ends | 0, and the Ball goes on |

You can earn up to 18 Hearts, and your total decides the ending:

| Hearts | Result |
|---|---|
| Under 8 | Bad ending: the rival wins him |
| 8 to 11 | The rival is Humbled |
| 12 to 14 | The rival is Disgraced |
| 15 or more | The rival is Ruined |

**Closers end the waltz on the spot.** That's the main tension: when he's just under the mark, do you close and bank it, or keep going for Swept and risk his next move? A closer played above her mark earns no Hearts for that waltz.

**Card types**

| Type | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Flamenco** | Big hits that add Fluster |
| **Waltz** | Poise, healing and Music |
| **Tango** | Unbalances him so every later card hits harder |
| **Minuet** | Grows stronger the lower his Composure falls. Soft whisper cancels his next move |
| **Secrets and hearsay** | Chain the same tag in a measure for bonus damage. The rival's weakness tag hits double |
| **Rhythms** | Last the whole waltz, such as Duende (Flamenco +4) or Slow Time (Music drains 1 slower) |
| **Closers** | Deep Curtsy and Final Bow end the waltz |
| **Encore** | A move so impressive the band plays on: +3 Music |
| **Quick step** | She's so light on her feet that she fits in more: +2 steps this measure and draw a card |
| **Graces and Prince's Favour** | Simple, reliable cards |

### Endings

| Result | Ending |
|---|---|
| Prince won, Stain 3 or less | **Good:** she keeps her promise and rules with clean hands |
| Prince won, Stain above 3 | **Dark:** she keeps her promise, but the dirty tactics that got her there have consequences. Her reign is corrupted from the start |
| His Defence holds | **Bad:** the prince stays out of reach and the rival wins him |
| Suspicion hits 100 | **Bad:** you are caught |
| Strain hits 8 | **Bad:** your mistress collapses |

After a bad ending, you retry from the start of the month or from an earlier save. Each bad ending is a real story scene, so losing still gives the player something new.

**The month stays the same on a retry.** Her moves and the wheel results stay fixed, so what you learned is your reward: "she strikes on day 5, and this time I'm ready." It also means reloading can't reroll a bad spin. In Godot, keep the month's random seed in the save file.

**Across all 5 rivals,** I'd add Stain up over the whole game and let that choose the final ending. Each rival then needs a good and dark version only of her own last scene, which keeps the writing load realistic.

### Each rival is harder than the last

Rival 1 is the easiest, and each one after her should test something new. Only raising his Defence won't work: in the simulation, every 2 extra points cost a careful bot about 10 to 12 points of win rate, so a number-only ramp turns into a wall by the third rival.

Each rival instead brings one new rule, plus a small stat increase.

| Rival | Prince's Defence | New pressure (examples for your writer to shape) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 26 | None. She teaches the base game |
| 2 | Slightly higher | Hides one move even when you scout |
| 3 | Higher | Has her own spy, so Suspicion rises faster |
| 4 | Higher | Protects one secret tag, so it does less at the Ball |
| 5 | Highest | Fights harder each dance, with press risk climbing faster |

The player should grow too, or later months will feel unfair. Contact ranks and unlocked actions can carry between months, which also gives servant relationships a long-term payoff. Each rival gets tuned separately, because Rival 2 can only be balanced once we know how strong a player is after Rival 1.

### Planned, not yet in the prototype

- **Rival personalities:** each rival haggles differently, such as favouring a tag or getting more dangerous each dance.
- **Contact stories:** give each servant small scenes and unique actions, like Persona's social links.

## Ball screen layout

The screen follows the Potionomics haggle layout, where the characters own the screen. Both cut-out characters stand large on each side, and they talk to each other in speech bubbles above the dance. His next move is a small thought bubble. The hand sits low enough to leave the dance clear, and the card the player points at grows from where it sits, so it never covers the couple. Your mistress's name and Fluster sit at bottom left, and the prince's plate, with Composure, Music and his stance, sits at bottom right beside End Measure and the Steps orb. Draw and used piles sit in the corners, from Slay the Spire. Her dialogue lines are also in `webapp/lines.js`.

The portraits and ballroom in `webapp/assets/reference` are other artists' work, used for private reference only, and must be replaced before anything is shared.

**Dance silhouettes:** the couple on stage is a still silhouette that changes with each move, and each card shows the same pose. The 8 poses in `webapp/assets/reference/poses` are stock placeholders, and `POSE_IMAGE` in `webapp/app.js` sets which pose each move uses.

**Character motion:** the portraits breathe gently using a simple stretch. In Godot, this can become a small rig with Skeleton2D and Polygon2D, or stay as a simple stretch.

**Key art moments:** Encore and each style's top move trigger a full-screen illustration, which stays up until the player closes it with the X button. When the prince's move gets past your Poise, his own illustration appears the same way, so fully blocking him skips it. His attack has its own placeholder sound, generated in code. The current image is another artist's work, used as a private placeholder. Each moment could get its own art later.

**Music:** each dance style can have its own song, crossfading between waltzes. Flamenco uses `Bailón de palmas`, and Waltz uses `Bailón de Tres — Accordion Waltz`, both made with Suno. The waltz is trimmed slightly so the two songs match in loudness. Tango and Minuet have no song yet, so the current song keeps playing during those stances. Music goes quieter during the rival's scene and card editing.

**Music plan:** each style's theme needs 3 versions that build through the round: the base version for waltz 1, a bit more intense for waltz 2, and the climax for waltz 3. That applies to Flamenco and Waltz first, then Tango and Minuet when they have themes.

**Future task:** remake the songs in 3/4 time, so the music moves like a waltz.

**Tutorial:** one round of 3 waltzes that teaches one idea at a time. Luna, a friendly talking cat, guides the player from a speech bubble, darkening the screen around each part she explains and waiting for the player to try it. She appears only in the tutorial, and her portrait is another artist's work, used as a placeholder. Waltz 1 covers Composure, the gold mark, Music, Steps, his next move, Poise, End Measure and the piles. Waltz 2 covers Hearts, stances, answering, phrases, Flourishes and Rhythm cards. Waltz 3 covers Fluster, Secrets, tags, the rival's weakness and Closers. The text is in `webapp/tutorial.js` for the writer to replace. The tutorial's Hearts are only practice, so it can't be lost.

**Sound effects:** every button, card hover, card play, silhouette pose change, block, Flourish and result has a sound. All are placeholders generated in `webapp/audio.js`, and the Sound button mutes them with the music.

**Later idea:** add a setting to turn key art moments on or off.

**Later idea:** add Tango and Minuet songs, and lock the dancers to each song's real beat.

## Rules that still need updating

The Ball changed after the month rules were written, so these older parts need rewiring:

- **Prince's Defence:** the month rules and the difficulty table still use it. Those effects should feed into the rival's mark or his starting Composure.
- **Stances in the Ball:** these now work as: Forward (his moves hit 50% harder), Guarded (your cards Charm 20% less), Proud (he recovers 3 Composure each measure), Aloof (1 fewer step each measure). Your 2nd move or better in the matching style cancels the stance for that waltz.
- **Court Dances and the masked ball:** these should become short versions of this new haggle.
- **The Python prototype:** it still plays the old Ball, so its numbers below describe the old rules. `webapp/` has the current Ball.

| Flamenco | 57% | 59% |

**What looks good**

- **Dirty play is tempting again.** Dark wins beat good wins for every style, by 8 to 14 points.
- **Lessons and the school pay off.** The best builds win more than twice as often as skipping lessons.
- **The wheel doesn't decide runs.** Removing its results changed a waltz build by about 1 point.
- **Tango and waltz are close,** and every build that reaches the Ball has a real chance.

**What needs work**

- **Flamenco and minuet are behind.** Flamenco wins 47%, and minuet collapses 44% of the time. Minuet's moves are strong, but bots can't keep the bars safe while training it.
- **The cost of specialising shrank.** It's now 5 to 9 points for most styles and missing for flamenco. Random draws hurt everyone, so stances may need to bite harder.
- **Dabbling fell to 39%.** With moves in a random deck, a few cheap cards rarely show up when needed. It may need a boost if it should be a valid way to play.
- **Spread a Rumour and Blackmail are untested.** No bot uses them, so nobody has shown they're worth it yet.
- **Court Dance length is unknown.** Three extra dances could make a month drag, and only playtesting will show that.

**What this can't tell us:** whether any of it is fun or addictive. Bots don't get hooked, and only people playing can answer that. These numbers also depend on simple scripts, and a good human should beat them.

## Showing the dance

The couple should dance on screen while the player chooses cards, using poses and not full animation.

- **Each move gets one couple pose.** When a card is played, the stage snaps to its pose with a sound and a small camera push, and the same drawing can serve as the card art.
- **The first version uses silhouettes.** Dark couple shapes against a bright ballroom need no faces or costumes, suit the masks, and are far quicker to draw.
- **Rough count for the proof of concept:** about 18 poses. That's 12 moves, 4 for the partner's stance moves, and 2 for winning and losing a dance.
- **Godot tools:** Sprite2D for the poses, Tween for the snap and camera push, and AnimationPlayer for a gentle idle sway.
- **Timing:** test it with stick figures at step 3 of the roadmap, then add real art once the dance plays well.

Avoid frame-by-frame animation. Two dancers across many moves and outcomes would take far too long.

## Development target

If this goes into development, the goal is one finished section: **the opening of the game, with 1 rival.** The other 4 rivals are out of scope until the opening is done and people want more.

**The opening includes:**

- The story set-up: who the helper is, what her mistress promised, and why the Ball matters
- One full month with the calendar, bars, intrigue, lessons and the rival's moves
- Court Dances and Fortune's Wheel
- The first Ball, and the rival's fall
- The good, dark and bad endings for this rival
- A hook at the end that points toward the next rival

**Done means:** someone who has never seen the game can play from the first scene to an ending without help, and wants to know what happens next.

**Left out for now:** rivals 2 to 5, carrying progress between months, and moves 4 to 6 in each style, which can't be reached in one month anyway.

**Scope to decide:** with only one month, the opening may not need all four dance styles. Teaching Flamenco and Waltz would reduce the writing and art, and those two already have themes. Tango and Minuet could arrive with later rivals.

## Roadmap

Each step ends in a test, and nothing moves forward until it passes.

| Step | Build | Pass when |
|---|---|---|
| **0. Paper test** | Play the terminal prototype and tune the numbers | You want another run after losing |
| **1. Learn Godot** | Godot's official "Your first 2D game" tutorial | You can explain scenes, nodes and signals |
| **2. Plain Godot version** | Bars, calendar, planning and a text log, with grey boxes for art | A full month plays start to finish |
| **3. The reveal** | Actions play out one by one with sound, motion, stat jumps and night teases | Results feel good with no story yet |
| **4. The Ball and Court Events** | The haggle, Dance Card, Court Dances, Fortune's Wheel, dance school, and saves | The haggle is tense without any story |
| **5. First writing and art pass** | Rival's story, key scenes, placeholder portraits | Testers care about the rival |
| **6. Playtest** | 5 or more people who don't know the game | Testers ask to play again unprompted |
| **7. Finish the opening** | Full story, final art and music, and polish for 1 rival | It meets the "done" test above |

The step 6 result is the real measure. Whether people restart without being asked tells you more than anything they say about it.

**Godot tip for step 2:** keep action data in Resources and not hard-coded in scripts. Then you can tune numbers, and the writer can add text, without touching game logic. Install the current stable Godot 4 release, as it isn't on this machine yet.

## Open questions

1. **Does the opening need all four dance styles?** Current pick: start with Flamenco and Waltz, and add Tango and Minuet with later rivals.
2. **What exactly does corruption look like in the dark ending?** Current pick: the writer builds it from what the player actually did, such as blackmail victims or rumours that return to haunt her reign.
3. **Is winning the prince enough of a payoff, or does the rival need her own big moment?** Current pick: the Ball ends with a scene of her ruin, so destroying her stays the thing to chase.
4. **Is 12 days right?** Current pick: yes for the proof of concept. Step 0 will show if it feels too short or too long.
5. **Should players see the exact Stain number?** Current pick: yes for now, because a choice only feels meaningful when you know its cost. The final game could show it as your mistress's growing unease instead.
6. **Should the month stay identical on every retry?** Current pick: yes, so retrying feels smarter and reloads can't reroll a spin.
